Aurral Pocket Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 19, 2026

Aurral Pocket ("the app", "we", "us"), shown as "Aurral" on your home screen, is an open-source mobile client for Aurral, a self-hosted music discovery and request manager for Lidarr. The app is published by Dylan Francis and distributed through the Apple App Store and Google Play. The source code is available at github.com/dylfrancis/aurral-pocket.

This policy explains what data the app handles, where that data goes, and what choices you have. The short version: the app does not operate any servers of its own, does not run analytics or tracking, and does not send your data to us. It talks to the Aurral server that you configure, and to a few other services only when you use the features that need them (see section 4).

Summary. Aurral Pocket connects to the self-hosted Aurral server address you enter. It contacts other services only for specific features: Apple's Shazam service and MusicBrainz when you identify a song, and your server's sign-in provider if your server uses single sign-on. On iPhone, it tells Siri your playlist names and what Siri played for you, so you can ask Siri to play your music. Credentials, your session token, and app preferences are stored on your device. No analytics, telemetry, advertising SDKs, or third-party trackers are included.

1. About the Aurral ecosystem

Aurral Pocket is a client; it is not a service. All music discovery, library browsing, search, and request management is performed by the Aurral server instance you run yourself (typically via Docker) and that you connect the app to by entering its URL.

Because Aurral is self-hosted, the operator of the Aurral server - not the authors of Aurral Pocket - controls how data on that server is stored, processed, and shared with any upstream services it integrates with (for example MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz, Koito, Lidarr, Navidrome). If you are connecting to someone else's Aurral server, ask them about their practices. If you are running your own, you are the operator.

2. Information the app handles

2.1 Information you provide

  • Server address. The URL of the Aurral server you want the app to connect to.
  • Account credentials. Your Aurral username and password, entered during sign-in to obtain a session token from your server. If your server uses single sign-on, you sign in on your identity provider's own page, shown inside the app, and the app receives only the session your Aurral server issues.
  • Optional custom headers. Request headers you add in Settings > Server, such as Cloudflare Access service tokens, for reaching a server behind a proxy. They are sent with every request to your server.
  • Optional listening service keys. A ListenBrainz user token or Koito API key you enter in Settings > Listening. The app sends it to your Aurral server, which uses it to scrobble your plays, and does not keep a copy on your device.
  • Optional ZIP / postal code. If you use the "Shows near you" discovery section and choose to enter a postal code instead of IP-based location, that value is stored on your device and sent to your Aurral server with nearby-show requests.
  • Search queries and interactions you submit while using the app (artist searches, requests, etc.) - these are sent to your Aurral server so it can respond to them.

2.2 Information stored on your device

The following is stored locally on your device. None of it is transmitted to the authors of Aurral Pocket.

  • Secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore, via expo-secure-store):
    • Session token issued by your Aurral server
    • Cached profile info returned by your server (user id, username, role)
    • Session expiry and last-active timestamps
    • Your username and password - only if you explicitly enable "Remember me"
    • Your biometric (Face ID / Touch ID) preference
    • Custom headers you added for your server
    • The name this phone uses for queue sync (see 2.3), which is never restored onto another phone
  • Local app storage (via AsyncStorage):
    • Your configured Aurral server URL
    • Recent search history (kept on-device; you can clear it from the search screen)
    • Nearby-location preference (IP-based or a ZIP / postal code you entered)
    • Your playback queue and queue sync state
    • Plays waiting to be sent to your server
    • The list of songs saved offline and of audio loaded ahead of time
    • App settings, such as theme, date and time format, views, Discover layout, and playback settings
    • Whether you signed in with single sign-on, and your provider's sign-out address
  • Files:
    • Songs you save offline
    • Tracks from your queue, loaded ahead of time so a drop in signal doesn't stop the music, up to the cache size you set in Settings > Playback and Storage
  • Sign-in web view: cookies your identity provider sets during single sign-on, kept in the app's own web storage and not shared with your browser.

Section 7 explains what signing out, changing server, and uninstalling remove. If your phone backs up to iCloud or Google, those backups can include the app's local data, under your Apple or Google account.

2.3 Information sent to your server as you listen

  • Plays. Each song you play is reported to your Aurral server: its title, artist, album, MusicBrainz identifiers, length, and when you played it. Your server keeps them as your listening history and passes them on to ListenBrainz or Koito if your account is linked to one. A play recorded while you're offline waits on your device until it can be sent.
  • Queue sync. Your playback queue is saved to your account on your Aurral server, so other Aurral players and Subsonic apps signed in to the same account can pick up where you left off. It's saved under a name made from your phone's name and a short random tag, for example "Aurral Pocket on Pixel 9 1a2b". On Android, the phone's name can be one you chose yourself.

2.4 Song identification

When you tap to identify a song, the app listens through the microphone for up to a minute, until it finds a match or you stop it. Apple's ShazamKit turns the sound into a fingerprint on your device and sends that fingerprint to Apple's Shazam service to match it. The app does not save or upload the recording itself, and it never listens unless you start it.

After a match, the app looks up the song's ISRC (an industry code for a recording) on MusicBrainz, to show the right artists. Matches are kept in memory to avoid repeat lookups and are not stored on your device.

2.5 Siri

On iPhone, you can ask Siri to play music in Aurral, for example "play Kid A in Aurral". Siri works out what you asked for and hands the app only that, such as an album or artist name. The app never receives your voice. To answer, it looks in the music saved on your phone, then searches your Aurral server, as a search in the app does. Apple handles your Siri requests under its privacy policy.

So that Siri hears your requests right and knows Aurral plays music, the app gives Siri on your iPhone:

  • the names of your playlists and flows
  • how many songs your Library holds
  • each song, album, artist or station Siri plays for you, with its title and artist

Signing out and changing server remove the playlist names and played items from Siri.

2.6 Information automatically collected

None by the app. Aurral Pocket does not embed any analytics, crash reporting, advertising, attribution, or tracking SDKs. It does not read advertising or hardware identifiers, contacts, calendars, photos, health data, or precise GPS location, and it uses the microphone only as described in 2.4.

Apple and Google independently collect aggregate install and crash information for apps distributed through the App Store and Google Play. That data is governed by Apple's privacy policy and Google's privacy policy, respectively, and the app authors do not have access to personally identifiable information from it.

3. How information is used

  • The server URL, token, and credentials are used solely to authenticate and communicate with the Aurral server you configured.
  • Custom headers are sent only to your Aurral server, to get past the proxy in front of it.
  • Recent searches and nearby-location preferences are used only to populate UI on your device.
  • Plays and your queue are sent to your Aurral server for your listening history, scrobbling if you linked a service, and picking up your queue on another device.
  • Microphone audio is used only to identify the song playing around you, while you are identifying one.
  • On iPhone, your playlist and flow names, your Library's size, and what Siri played are given to Siri only so it can understand and play your requests.
  • Biometric authentication (Face ID / Touch ID) is used only to re-unlock a saved session on your device. Biometric data itself never leaves your device - iOS and Android handle the match and only return a yes/no result to the app.

4. Network connections

The app makes network requests to:

  • Your Aurral server, at the URL you provide, for authentication, discovery, library, search, requests, streaming, artwork, play history, queue sync, and live updates.
  • Any hosts that your Aurral server points images and audio to (for example, cover art from upstream music metadata providers, or preview clips hosted by Deezer). The app does not choose these hosts; it only loads URLs returned by your server.
  • Apple's Shazam service, only while you identify a song, to match the audio fingerprint described in 2.4. Apple handles that data under its privacy policy.
  • MusicBrainz (musicbrainz.org), only after a song is identified, to look up the song's ISRC. The request carries only that code and the app's name, nothing about your account, though MusicBrainz sees your IP address as any website does. It is run by the MetaBrainz Foundation under its privacy policy.
  • Your server's identity provider, only if your server uses single sign-on. Signing in shows the provider's page inside the app, and signing out opens its sign-out page in the background. Your server's operator chooses the provider.

Links you tap, such as concert tickets or artist pages on MusicBrainz and Last.fm, open in your browser, outside the app.

The app does not contact any servers operated by the authors of Aurral Pocket, and does not contact any third-party analytics or advertising networks.

5. Device permissions

  • Face ID / Touch ID / biometrics (optional). Used only to re-authenticate an existing session. You can decline this permission and continue using password sign-in.
  • Microphone (optional). Asked for the first time you identify a song, and used only while identifying one (see 2.4). If you decline, everything else in the app still works, and you can change your answer in your phone's settings.
  • Siri (iPhone). The app doesn't ask for a Siri permission: Siri can play from Aurral once you're signed in (see 2.5). You can turn Siri off for the app in your phone's settings.
  • Location. The app does not request device location. The "Shows near you" feature uses IP-based geolocation performed by your Aurral server, or a postal code you type in - never GPS.
  • Contacts, Photos, Calendar, Health, Motion. Not requested.

6. Data sharing

The app authors do not receive, store, or share your data. The app sends data to the Aurral server whose address you enter, and, only for the features described in section 4, to Apple's Shazam service, MusicBrainz, and your server's identity provider. On iPhone, it also gives Siri what section 2.5 lists. How your server uses or forwards data (for example, to Lidarr, MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz, Koito, or Navidrome) is determined by its operator and configuration, not by the app.

7. Data retention and deletion

  • Signing out removes your session token, profile info, playback queue, audio loaded ahead of time, and the playlist names and played items given to Siri. It keeps your server address, custom headers, saved sign-in (if you chose "Remember me"), songs saved offline, and plays not yet sent, which are sent when the same account signs back in.
  • Changing server (Settings > Server > Change Server) also removes your server address, custom headers, saved sign-in, songs saved offline, and plays not yet sent. The app does the same after 30 days without use. App settings and recent searches stay until you clear them or uninstall.
  • Uninstalling removes all app-stored data from the device. Copies in an iCloud or Google backup follow that backup's own retention.
  • Data on your Aurral server, including your listening history and saved queue, is retained according to the operator's configuration. If you run your own server, you control retention. If you use someone else's, contact that operator.

8. Children's privacy

Aurral Pocket is not directed to children under 13, and the authors do not knowingly collect information from them - in fact, the authors collect no information from any user. Parents or guardians who believe a child's information may have been stored on an Aurral server should contact that server's operator.

9. Security

Sensitive values (session token, saved credentials if you enable "Remember me", custom headers, session metadata) are stored using the operating system's secure keystore. On iPhone, the session token, profile info, custom headers, and last-active time can be read while the phone is locked, once it has been unlocked since it started, so Siri can play music from a locked phone. Your saved password can still be read only while the phone is unlocked. Traffic between the app and your Aurral server is only as secure as the address you configure; we strongly recommend using HTTPS.

No system is perfectly secure. You are responsible for keeping the device on which the app is installed physically secure and up to date.

10. Your choices

  • Sign out from Settings to clear the session token on the device.
  • Disable "Remember me" and biometric sign-in from the sign-in screen to avoid storing credentials on the device.
  • Clear recent searches from the search screen.
  • Decline microphone access, or turn it off later in your phone's settings. Only song identification needs it.
  • On iPhone, turn Siri off for the app in your phone's settings.
  • Remove anything you saved offline with "Remove from Phone", and empty the audio loaded ahead of time with Clear Cache in Settings > Playback and Storage.
  • Change server to remove everything tied to your server and account from the device.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all app-stored data from your device.
